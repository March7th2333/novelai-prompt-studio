# Visual design playbook — authored proposals, not model guarantees

## Minimum visual brief

Identify subject, action, framing, viewing direction, environment, dominant light, palette, medium, background density, and a focal hierarchy. Fill only what helps the request. Treat unrequested specifics as proposed choices, not recovered facts.

Convert “dreamlike” into selectable choices such as low-contrast depth, softened distant edges, limited iridescent highlights, or impossible but coherent lighting. Convert “less plastic” into a testable change in highlights, surface roughness or visible brush texture. Do not equate “high-end” with one mandatory style.

## Default economy

Use one primary medium and one compatible finish. Establish a principal bright region and supporting accents. When the brief says sparse, leave space instead of adding every attractive accessory. Composition cues can conflict across scopes: full-body versus tight close-up, daylight versus a night-only brief, or a requested flat poster versus strong volumetric rendering. Flag ambiguity before treating any pair as universally incompatible.

## Opt-in style cards

`presets/styles.json` stores draft suggestions. Cards must be selected explicitly or because the current request clearly matches them. They never override must-keep details. Camera accessories, crystals, star trails, and specific hair colors are not global defaults.

## Reference images

Extract only observable features and separate transferable aspects: composition, lighting, palette, materials, styling. When asked for “lighting only,” do not also copy facial identity, pose or clothes. Distinguish an observed soft shadow from an inferred light source. Record uncertainty. Do not recover invisible generation metadata from pixels.
